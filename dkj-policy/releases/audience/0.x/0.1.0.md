# Release notes v0.1.0 🚀

**Date:** 2026-10-27\
**Type:** Minor\
**For whom:** consumers of this product, and colleagues in the organisation -- one section each

## What changed

This is the library's first release, so it covers everything built so far.

**Your collection has a home of its own.** All 12,471 tracks sit in the library's own database, with
fields of your own beside the Spotify data, and that database comes along with every clone, so another
machine has the whole collection without syncing first. Each track has a stable, readable ID per artist,
and each artist has an ID of its own.

**The track register is where you work.** One page to search, filter and sort the whole collection,
without a login or a sync. Sort with one click on a column header; a title that starts with a punctuation
mark sorts under its first letter. Filter by search term, year or year range, BPM, genre, album and group,
all in one framed Filters box. The register remembers your filters, sort order and columns when you come
back, and "Filters wissen" clears them. The Previous and Next buttons always fit on screen, and the
register shows only your own collection, without the eight hundred-odd tracks from other people's
playlists.

**Most fields fill themselves.** Album (8,257 tracks), BPM (7,454), genre and group are derived from your
playlists, so you no longer fill them in by hand. Each track lists every artist, puts the remixer first
where you order it that way, shows its desktop file name and links to the playlists it is in. Titles are
clean, without Radio Mix, Remastered or featuring, and live variants are merged, so every song appears
once.

**You can rate tracks.** Click the pencil in the register to give a track a rating from `tier-1` to
`tier-8`, saved at once and sortable. Each pair of tiers has its own symbol: a green circle, a blue
triangle, a purple diamond or an orange pentagon. Your Top 100 tracks start on the highest tier.

**See what you play most.** The playcount column shows how often you played each track, filled from your
Spotify streaming history, and you can sort on it.

**Years are right.** A year column shows when each song came out. On compilations and reissues the year is
the original's, for example *I'm a Believer*: 1966 rather than 2008. A wrong year for a well-known song
can be pinned with one line.

**Every page is one click from where you expect it.** The app opens on a home page with a card for every
part of the library. Spotify has its own page, and all six playlist pages sit behind its Playlists card:
the Playlist manager, Music Mood Colours, DJ Cylow, Privé, Maple Classic 2026 LAN and Classic Pop. Classic
Pop shows the whole collection across every colour playlist and where each song sits. A breadcrumb bar on
every page shows where you are, and old bookmarks redirect to the new addresses.

**It looks like the other DKJ-Solutions apps.** The library uses the house style, opens in Roze, and
takes any of the twelve house colours from the settings panel, where "Full" now widens the Spotify pages
too.

**It works anywhere in the house.** Open the app from another device on your network and sync works there
too. A session also starts with the app already open beside it, without `npm run dev`.

## What it is worth

- **The collection stops being a Spotify export and becomes your own.** Fields Spotify does not have
  (rating, genre, group, BPM, play count) live beside its data and travel with the repo. The music
  library is now the place you curate in, not a view of somebody else's.
- **No hand-tagging pass before the register was useful.** Album, BPM, genre and group were derived
  from the way the playlists were already organised, for thousands of tracks at once, so the register
  was usable on day one.
- **One app, reachable from any device, that looks like the rest of DKJ-Solutions.** Shared styling and
  LAN access mean no local look to maintain and no fixed machine to work from.
